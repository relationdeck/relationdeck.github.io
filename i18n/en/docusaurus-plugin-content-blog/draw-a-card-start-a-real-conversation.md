When was the last time you talked with someone close to you about something other than "how have you been?"

We noticed that a lot of conversations stay on the surface — not because there's nothing to say, but because nobody has a good way in. That way in is what RelationDeck sets out to give you.

<!-- truncate -->

## The question is harder than the answer

Over dinner with friends, we mostly talk about work, the show we're watching, where to go next time. Nothing wrong with any of that — but after three hours of it, you often get home and realise you have no idea what's actually been on your friend's mind.

You'd like to ask something deeper, but it feels too sudden. "What's the last thing that really moved you?" is hard to drop naturally into small talk.

Read off a card, though, it's much easier. The card is asking, not you — and the person answering relaxes too.

## What happened at one dinner

At one get-together with friends, we opened the app and started drawing cards. Everyone was a little awkward at first. The first question was "What's something you've wanted to do but keep putting off?"

The first person answered briefly. The second said, "I've had something like that…" and went on to tell a story none of us had ever heard. We ended up talking late into the night.

The card didn't do anything special. It just gave everyone a question they could pick up and keep going with.

## 12 decks for 12 relationships

Different relationships call for different questions. "What part of yourself are you most afraid people will see?" is too soon for someone you've just met, and "What's your favorite kind of weather?" is too shallow for your partner. So the app is split into 12 decks by relationship, thirty cards each:

### Self Reflection

Sit with yourself, and ask. For example: "What do you pretend not to care about?"

### Icebreaker

First meetings, beyond small talk. For example: "What topic gets you excited the moment it comes up?"

### Friends

New stories from old friends, and new friends who feel close fast. For example: "If you could say one thing to yourself five years ago, what would it be?"

### Dating

Let a card speak first, and get closer question by question. For example: "What little things make you feel cared about?"

### Couples

Not just the day — dreams, fears and gratitude too. For example: "When you're stressed, what do I do that helps most?"

### Life Together

Love is the easy part. Living together takes talking — money, chores, family and the future, out on the table. For example: "Do you think the way we split the housework is fair?"

### Talk to Parents

A grown child asking their parents, so every generation's story gets told. For example: "What was your biggest dream when you were young?"

### Talk to Kids

A parent asking a child, with questions a kid can answer, so you hear what they're really thinking. For example: "When things get hard, how would you like me to help?"

### Siblings

Same home, different memories. For example: "How do you think your place in the family — oldest, middle, youngest — shaped who you are?"

### Workplace

See the person, and the work gets easier. For example: "What pace of work brings out your best?"

### Managing Up

Someone asking their manager or mentor, to get to know who leads them. For example: "What did the best manager you ever had do right?"

### 1-on-1

A manager asking a report, building trust one honest conversation at a time. For example: "What could I do to make your work easier?"

Every card is also marked with its depth: Light, Deeper or Deepest. Light questions are dealt first, and the deeper ones come once everyone has loosened up. To skip a question, just swipe left.

For when to use each deck and more sample questions, see [Card Resources](/explore).

## After the talk, write it down

The two features we use most are notes and People.

A card can hold several notes, each tagged with who you talked with. If three people answer the same question at dinner, that's three notes. Later, under Records → People, tap a friend to see everything you've talked about together. Flip through it before you next meet, and it's easy to pick up where you left off.

Review, in the Records tab, sums up the last 30 days: which relationships you talked about most, how deep you usually went, and who you haven't talked with in a while. It describes and never scores — more of a mirror than a report card. There's also a streak and a conversation calendar, so you can look back at which questions came up on which day. If you want to make it a habit, turn on the daily reminder to get one question at the same time each day.

All of this stays on your phone. No account, and nothing is uploaded to any server by default.

## Try it tonight

Next time you sit down to eat with friends, your partner or family, put the phone on the table, draw a card, read the question out loud — and really listen to the answer.

Nothing to prepare. The first question is usually the hardest; it gets easier after that.
